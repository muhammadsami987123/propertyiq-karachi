Methodology

PropertyIQ is built on the principle that a number is only useful if you know how confident to be in it. This page explains how our data is classified, how confidence is calculated, and where the honest limits are.

Data types

Every figure in PropertyIQ is tagged with a data type. We do not blend these silently — the type is always visible next to the number it describes.

Verified

Sourced from a documented transaction, registered listing, or an authoritative third-party record we can trace to origin. Verified figures carry the highest weight in any comparison or calculation.

Research estimate

Derived from multiple listings, agent input, or aggregated public sources, cross-checked for plausibility but not independently verified against a single authoritative record. This is the majority of what you will see on the platform, and it is labeled as such rather than presented as fact.

Demo

Placeholder or illustrative data used where real observations do not yet exist for a locality or property type. Demo data is never used to support an investment decision and is always marked as demo, never blended with real figures.

How confidence is determined

Each market record carries a confidence level — High, Medium, or Low — computed from five factors:

  1. Observations — how many independent data points support the figure. A range built from a handful of listings is treated with more caution than one built from dozens.
  2. Source quality — whether the source is a registered transaction, an established listing platform, an agent estimate, or an unverifiable secondary reference.
  3. Freshness — how recently the data was collected or updated. Karachi's market moves quickly; a figure from two years ago is treated differently than one from last month.
  4. Geographic specificity — whether the figure applies to the exact locality, or is inferred from a wider town-level or district-level average.
  5. Consistency — how closely independent observations agree with each other. Wide disagreement between sources lowers confidence even with many observations.

The confidence explanation attached to each locality's market data spells out which of these factors drove the rating for that specific figure — it is not a generic disclaimer.

Limitations

  • Karachi does not have a centralized, publicly queryable property transaction registry. Every figure here is built from the best available secondary evidence, not from a ground-truth ledger.
  • Historical trend lines are only shown where enough consistent observations exist over time. Where they don't, the platform says so explicitly rather than drawing a misleading line through sparse data.
  • Market categorizations (e.g. "high-growth", "stable") are relative to other tracked Karachi localities, not an absolute national or international benchmark.
  • PropertyIQ is an intelligence layer, not a valuation service or a substitute for a licensed surveyor, lawyer, or financial advisor before a transaction.
